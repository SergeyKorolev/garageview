## Read an Advertisement as a Set of Claims

When examining a car advertisement, it is crucial to interpret it as a collection of claims rather than verified facts. This approach helps you critically evaluate the information presented and identify what is stated, what is supported by evidence, and what remains unknown. Here's how to break it down:

| Stated Claim | Supported By What | Still Unknown |
|--------------|-------------------|---------------|
| "Low mileage" | Odometer photo | Service history to verify accuracy |
| "One owner" | Seller's statement | Documentation proving ownership |
| "Accident-free" | No visible damage in photos | Detailed accident history report |
| "Full service history" | Scanned service book pages | Completeness and authenticity of records |

When reading an advertisement, note each claim and consider what evidence is provided to support it. For example, a claim of "low mileage" might be backed by a photo of the odometer, but without a service history, you cannot confirm its accuracy. Similarly, a statement of "one owner" is only as reliable as the documentation provided. Always be aware of what is not mentioned, as missing information can be as telling as what is included.

## Check Identity Before Details

Before delving into the specifics of a car listing, ensure that the identity of the vehicle is clear and accurate. This involves verifying that the car shown is the exact one being sold, rather than a stock photo or a configuration example. Pay attention to the following aspects:

- **Photos**: Compare the photos with the description to ensure they match. Look for unique identifiers such as scratches or specific features that confirm the car's identity.
- **Reference/Source/Date**: Check the reference number, the source of the listing, and the date it was posted. This information helps verify the listing's currency and authenticity.
- **First Registration vs. Build Year**: These are often confused or used interchangeably, but they are distinct. The first registration date indicates when the car was first registered for use, while the build year refers to when it was manufactured. If unclear, ask the seller for clarification rather than making assumptions.

By confirming these details, you can avoid misunderstandings and ensure that the car you are interested in is accurately represented.

## Price Basis

Understanding the price basis of a car listing is essential for making informed decisions. Advertisements often present prices in various forms, such as vehicle cash price, deposit, monthly payments, or net and gross amounts. Here's how to interpret these:

- **Vehicle Cash Price**: This is the total amount required to purchase the car outright. It should be clearly stated in the listing.
- **Deposit and Monthly Payments**: If the car is available through financing, the listing may include a required deposit and monthly payment amounts. Ensure these figures are clear and understand that they do not represent the total cost.
- **Net vs. Gross**: Some listings might show net prices (excluding VAT) and gross prices (including VAT). Be sure to note which is being presented, especially if VAT is applicable in your region.

Always preserve the currency and units as stated in the advertisement. Avoid making automatic assumptions about exchange rates or conversions, as these can vary.

## Crosscheck Headline, Body, Fields, and Photos

To ensure the accuracy and completeness of a car listing, crosscheck the information presented in the headline, body text, fields, and photos. This process helps identify inconsistencies and missing information:

- **Headline vs. Body**: The headline might highlight key features, but the body text should provide more detailed information. Ensure these sections align and do not contradict each other.
- **Fields**: Listings often include fields for specific details such as mileage, engine size, and fuel type. Verify that these fields match the descriptions and photos.
- **Photos**: Use photos to confirm the presence of features mentioned in the listing. If a feature is missing from the photos, it does not necessarily mean it is absent; it may simply be unpictured.

Remember, omitted information such as accident history does not imply a clean bill of health. If accident history is not mentioned, it is crucial to inquire directly with the seller. By thoroughly crosschecking all elements of the listing, you can better assess the vehicle's true condition and value.

## Three Fictional Advertisement Puzzles

When browsing car listings, it's crucial to identify potential discrepancies or missing information that could impact your decision. Here are three fictional advertisement puzzles to consider:

### A. Mileage Discrepancy

In this scenario, an advertisement headline states that the car has 48,000 km, but the body of the ad mentions 68,000 km. This inconsistency raises questions about the car's actual mileage.

- **Clue:** Headline states 48,000 km; body states 68,000 km.
- **Unknown:** The car's true mileage.
- **Exact Question:** What is the current odometer reading, and can you provide evidence?
- **Next Step:** Contact the seller to request a photo of the odometer and any service records that confirm the mileage. This will help verify the actual distance the car has traveled.

### B. Ambiguous Parking Package

The advertisement mentions a "parking package," but the accompanying photo does not clearly show a rear camera or sensors. It's important not to assume the presence of specific features without confirmation.

- **Clue:** Text mentions "parking package"; photo lacks clarity.
- **Unknown:** The specific features included in the parking package.
- **Exact Question:** What functions are included in the parking package, and can you provide clear photos of the vehicle's equipment?
- **Next Step:** Ask the seller for a detailed list of features included in the parking package and request additional photos that clearly show these features. This ensures you understand what you're purchasing.

### C. Incomplete New Car Offer

An advertisement headline offers a new car for 250 EUR per month, but it lacks information about the full vehicle price or configuration. This makes it difficult to compare with a cash offer.

- **Clue:** Headline states 250 EUR per month; no full price or configuration details.
- **Unknown:** The total cost and specific configuration of the vehicle.
- **Exact Question:** What is the full purchase price and configuration of the vehicle?
- **Next Step:** Contact the dealership to request a detailed breakdown of the total cost, including any additional fees, and a complete list of the vehicle's specifications. This will allow you to compare the offer with other purchasing options.

## Prioritise Contradiction by Decision Impact

When evaluating car listings, prioritize contradictions that have a significant impact on your decision. Issues related to the price basis or essential features should take precedence over less critical details like color. This approach helps ensure that you focus on the most important aspects of the purchase.

### Price Basis or Essential Feature

Inconsistencies in the price basis or essential features can significantly affect the overall value and suitability of the car. For example, a discrepancy in mileage or missing information about included features can alter the perceived value of the vehicle.

- **Plausible Explanation:** The seller may have made an error in the listing, or there might be a misunderstanding about the vehicle's features or condition.
- **Evidence Contains Date and Source:** Ensure that any evidence you gather, such as photos or service records, includes a date and indicates which version of the advertisement it pertains to. This helps establish a timeline and context for any discrepancies.

By focusing on these critical contradictions, you can make more informed decisions and avoid potential pitfalls in the car-buying process. Always seek clarification and evidence from the seller to resolve any uncertainties before proceeding with a purchase.

## Translated Advertisements

When dealing with translated car advertisements, it's important to retain the original terms used by the seller. This is because technical terms may not have direct equivalents in other languages, and assumptions about their meaning can lead to misunderstandings.

For example, if an advertisement mentions "ABS" (Anti-lock Braking System), do not assume it is equivalent to any similar system without confirmation. Instead, ask the seller for plain explanations of the function and request vehicle-specific documentation that confirms the presence and condition of these features.

### Actions to Take

- **Retain Original Terms**: Keep terms like "ABS" or "ESP" as they are.
- **Request Plain Function Descriptions**: Ask the seller to explain what these features do.
- **Seek Documentation**: Request manuals or service records that verify the presence and functionality of these features.

This approach helps ensure you understand what features the vehicle truly offers and their condition.

## Photos: What You Can and Cannot Infer

Photos in car listings can be misleading if not carefully analyzed. Stock photos, for example, may not represent the actual vehicle being sold. Additionally, the absence of certain details in photos does not necessarily indicate a problem, but it does warrant further investigation.

### What to Consider

- **Stock Photo Possibility**: Check if the photos are generic or specific to the vehicle.
- **Detail Absence**: Missing details in photos should prompt questions, not assumptions.
- **Cropped Images**: Cropped photos do not automatically mean hidden faults, but they do require you to ask for more comprehensive images.

### Actions to Take

- **Request Current Photos**: Ask for recent photos showing the vehicle from various angles.
- **Operation or Condition Checks**: Arrange for a physical inspection or request a video demonstrating the vehicle's operation.

This ensures you have a clearer picture of the vehicle's actual condition.

## Documents and Seller Answers

Documents provided by the seller, such as service records or registration papers, should be carefully examined. It's important to note which car the documents pertain to, the date they were issued, and the specific claims they support.

### Key Considerations

- **Which Car**: Ensure documents match the vehicle in question.
- **What Date**: Check the dates on documents to confirm their relevance.
- **Which Claim**: Identify which seller claims are supported by these documents.

### Actions to Avoid

- **Publishing Personal Details**: Do not share personal information from documents.
- **Assuming Complete History**: A document does not necessarily prove the entire history of the vehicle.

### Next Steps

- **Clarify with Seller**: Ask the seller to clarify any discrepancies or missing information.
- **Verify with Authorities**: If possible, verify the documents with relevant authorities or databases.

By following these guidelines, you can better assess the reliability of the information provided and make more informed decisions about potential purchases.

## Move from Reading to a Seller Conversation

When you spot a potential car listing that piques your interest, it's crucial to initiate a conversation with the seller to clarify any contradictions or uncertainties. Start with a concise message that directly addresses any discrepancies you've noticed in the listing. For example, if the mileage stated in the description doesn't match the odometer reading in the photos, mention this inconsistency and ask for clarification.

Example Message:
"Hello, I noticed the listing states the car has 50,000 km, but the odometer photo shows 75,000 km. Could you please clarify this discrepancy? Additionally, could you provide any service records or documentation to verify the mileage?"

By asking for evidence, such as service records or maintenance logs, you can gain a clearer understanding of the car's history and condition. Keep your message polite and straightforward to encourage a positive response from the seller.

## Decide: Contact, View, or Pause

After gathering information from the seller, decide whether to proceed with a contact, schedule a viewing, or pause your interest in the listing. If a specific essential unknown, such as the car's accident history, warrants further questioning, continue the conversation with the seller. However, if a must-have feature remains unresolved, it may be wise to pause your interest until the seller provides the necessary information.

For condition matters that are difficult to assess through photos or descriptions, consider arranging a professional inspection. This step is particularly important for used cars, where unseen issues could significantly impact the vehicle's value and performance.

## Four FAQ

**1. How can I trust a polished ad?**
A polished ad can be appealing, but it's important to verify the details independently. Look for inconsistencies in the description and photos, and ask the seller for documentation to support their claims. Use the guide on [questions for car sellers](/en/guides/questions-for-car-sellers/) to ensure you cover all bases.

**2. What should I do if equipment is missing from the listing?**
If the listing lacks information about specific equipment or features, reach out to the seller for clarification. Ask for a detailed list of included equipment and any optional extras. This will help you assess the car's value and suitability for your needs.

**3. How do I handle a year conflict in the listing?**
Ask which date each field describes: manufacture, model year or first registration. Request documentation tied to the exact vehicle and have unexplained differences clarified. Keep the dates separate in your notes; do not infer that they must be identical or assume that a quick VIN lookup resolves every discrepancy.

**4. What if the ad is removed before I get answers?**
If an ad is removed before you receive the information you need, it may indicate the car has been sold or the seller is no longer interested in selling.
