## Where Should You Start Looking?

You can look for both new and used cars on [mobile.de](https://www.mobile.de/), [AutoScout24](https://www.autoscout24.com/) and [Otomoto](https://www.otomoto.pl/). Start with a geographic area you can realistically visit and a buying brief you can repeat across sources. These are starting points for finding advertisements; the suitability and availability of each offer still need checking.

### Mobile.de

Use mobile.de to run your first version of the brief if its results cover the area you can visit. Inspect both the structured fields and the seller’s description. Keep the search URL and filters so you can repeat the query or compare it with another source. A result is a candidate for screening, not proof that the vehicle meets every requirement.

### AutoScout24

Try the equivalent brief on AutoScout24 and check which filters carry the same meaning. Review whether it adds distinct suitable candidates, rather than simply more advertisements of cars you already found. Keep the currency, location and offer conditions visible when moving between searches.

### Otomoto

Use Otomoto as another source for new and used candidates, particularly when a search in Poland fits your plans. Apply the same essentials and record any filter differences. If a result is in another currency or uses unfamiliar wording, keep the original information alongside your interpretation and resolve it before comparing the offer.

When choosing a platform, consider the geographical area you are willing to visit for inspection and purchase. This approach ensures that you are not limited by unsupported portals or distant locations that may complicate the buying process.

## Build a Repeatable Search

To streamline your car search, it's crucial to establish a repeatable process that considers your specific needs and budget. Start by identifying your passenger and luggage requirements, as these will influence the type of car you need. For instance, if you frequently travel with family or carry large loads, you may prioritize a vehicle with ample seating and cargo space.

Next, decide if an automatic transmission is essential for you. This preference will significantly narrow down your options and should be one of the first filters you apply.

Set a maximum advertised price for your search, keeping in mind that additional unquoted fees such as taxes, registration, and insurance may apply. It's important to separate these costs from the advertised price to avoid exceeding your budget.

Log each exact filter you use and the date of your search. This record will help you track changes in availability and pricing over time. Remember that translated filters may not be identical across platforms, so verify that your criteria are correctly applied.

Consider running separate searches for new-stock, new-order, and used vehicles if these categories are relevant to your needs. This approach allows you to compare options and make informed decisions based on availability and pricing.

## Read the First Results Before Widening

Once you have set up your search filters, take the time to carefully read the first set of results. Inspect the relevance of each listing to your criteria and look for evidence of the car's condition, such as photos and detailed descriptions.

If the initial results do not meet your expectations, consider changing one optional constraint at a time. For example, you might adjust the price range slightly or expand the geographical area. However, keep your essential criteria intact to ensure that the results remain relevant.

Allocate a specific 30-minute session for your search process: spend 5 minutes setting up your search, 10 minutes reviewing results from the first source, 10 minutes on a second source, and 5 minutes taking notes. This structured approach helps maintain focus and efficiency, allowing you to make progress without becoming overwhelmed.

## Worked Example: Three Promising Advertisements

When searching for a car that meets specific criteria, it's essential to evaluate advertisements carefully. Let's consider a fictional buyer looking for an automatic car with enough boot space for a pushchair, within a visitable radius, and a price ceiling of 25,000 EUR. Here are three advertisements that seem promising at first glance:

### Advertisement A

- **Price Basis:** 24,000 EUR
- **Evidence:** This used car is advertised as automatic and includes a photograph of the boot. However, the advertisement does not confirm whether a pushchair can fit in the boot.
- **Next Action:** Contact the seller to inquire specifically about the boot space and whether it can accommodate a pushchair. It's also wise to ask for additional photos or measurements of the boot to make an informed decision.

### Advertisement B

- **Price Basis:** 3,000 EUR deposit
- **Evidence:** This is a new-stock advertisement, but it only lists the deposit amount, not the full price. Without knowing the total cost, it's impossible to determine if it fits within the budget.
- **Next Action:** Reach out to the dealer to request the full price of the vehicle. Clarify if the deposit is part of a financing plan and what the terms are. This will help assess whether the car is affordable and meets the buyer's financial criteria.

### Advertisement C

- **Price Basis:** Suspected Duplicate
- **Evidence:** This advertisement matches the seller reference, mileage, and photos of Advertisement A on another source. This suggests it might be a duplicate listing of the same car.
- **Next Action:** Verify the identity of the car by contacting the seller and confirming details such as the vehicle identification number (VIN) or other unique identifiers. This will help determine if it's indeed the same car as Advertisement A or a separate listing.

## Turn a Search Result into Evidence

When you find a promising car advertisement, it's crucial to document it thoroughly to avoid confusion and ensure you have all necessary information at your fingertips. Here's how to turn a search result into actionable evidence:

1. **Save the URL and Date:** Always save the URL of the advertisement and note the date you accessed it. This helps track changes in the listing and provides a reference if you need to revisit the ad later.

2. **Record the Seller Reference:** Many advertisements include a seller reference number or code. Record this information to easily identify and discuss the specific car with the seller.

3. **Note Exact Uncertainty:** If there are uncertainties in the advertisement, such as unclear pricing or missing feature confirmations, make a note of these. This will guide your questions when contacting the seller.

4. **Understand Database Fields Can Be Wrong:** Be aware that fields in online car databases can sometimes be incorrect or outdated. Features like transmission type or boot size might be misrepresented, so always verify these details directly with the seller.

5. **Monitor Source Changes:** Advertisements can change over time, with sellers updating prices, photos, or descriptions. Regularly check back on saved listings to see if any updates have been made that might affect your decision.

## Local and Cross-Border Searches

When searching for cars, consider both local and cross-border options. Each has its own set of practicalities to consider:

### Local Searches

Local searches are generally more straightforward. You can easily visit the dealership or seller, inspect the car, and take it for a test drive. Language barriers are minimal, and you can quickly address any issues or questions in person.

### Cross-Border Searches

Cross-border searches can expand your options but come with additional considerations:

- **Visit Practicalities**: Determine if you can feasibly travel to inspect the car. Consider travel time, costs, and logistics.
- **Language Barriers**: Ensure you can communicate effectively with the seller. Use translation services if necessary.
- **Inspection**: Plan how you will inspect the car. Consider hiring a local mechanic or using a third-party inspection service.
- **Collection**: Arrange for transportation of the car back to your home country. This may involve additional costs and logistics.

For more information on cross-border car purchases, including VAT considerations, visit the [Your Europe VAT car page](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/vehicles/cars/vat-buying-selling-cars/index_en.htm).

## What to Do When Search is Empty or Noisy

If your search results are not yielding useful options, it's important to refine your approach:

### Separate Impossible Constraints

Review your search criteria and identify any constraints that may be too restrictive. Adjust these to broaden your search and increase the number of potential matches.

### Sparse Descriptions

If listings lack detail, reach out to sellers for more information. Ask specific questions about the car's condition, history, and features to fill in the gaps.

### Wrong Condition

Ensure that your search filters are set correctly for the condition you want, whether new or used. Double-check that you haven't accidentally included the wrong category.

### Duplicates

Identify and remove duplicate listings from your search results. This will help you focus on unique options and avoid confusion.

### Expired Offers

Check the date of the listings to ensure they are still valid. Remove any expired offers from your log to keep your search up-to-date.

## Keep a Shortlist That Leads to Action

When searching for a car, it's crucial to maintain a shortlist of 3-5 candidates. This shortlist should not be a scientific optimum but rather a collection of vehicles that stand out with distinct identities and specific questions that need answering. The goal is to stop the initial search once you have these candidates, allowing you to focus on deeper research and decision-making. If all candidates fail to meet essential criteria, you can resume your search.

To create an effective shortlist, consider the following steps:

1. **Identify Distinct Features**: Look for cars that offer unique features or benefits that align with your needs. This could be anything from fuel efficiency to advanced safety features.

2. **Formulate Specific Questions**: For each car, note down specific questions that need answering. This could include queries about the vehicle's service history, accident records, or the authenticity of the seller's claims.

3. **Record Manual Notes**: Keep manual notes on each candidate's status. This includes any follow-up actions, such as scheduling a test drive or requesting additional information from the seller.

4. **Evaluate Essentials**: Ensure that each car meets your essential criteria, such as budget, size, and intended use. If none of the candidates meet these essentials, it's time to expand your search.

## FAQs

### Can AI Choose Where to Search Without Live Data?

AI can suggest general starting points from your location and buying brief. Without current source data, it cannot establish which portal has the most suitable cars available today. Treat a source recommendation as a search plan to test, and ask it to distinguish general knowledge from current evidence.

### Should New Cars Be Excluded?

No. All three marketplaces include new and used offers. Compare a specific new-stock or order quotation with specific used candidates against the same brief. Confirm the configuration, price conditions and delivery date; a “new” label alone does not establish that it fits better.

### Does Two Ads Mean Two Cars?

Not necessarily. Two ads could refer to the same car listed by different sellers or platforms. Always verify the details and cross-reference the information to ensure you are not duplicating your efforts.

### Should a Wide Country Search Come First?

A wide country search can be beneficial if you are open to importing a car or if local options are limited. However, consider the logistics, potential import taxes, and registration requirements before expanding your search beyond your immediate area.
