## Compare Actual Offers Rather Than the Labels New and Used

When considering a car purchase, it's crucial to look beyond the simple labels of "new" and "used" and focus on the specifics of each offer. This approach ensures you understand what you're truly getting and allows for a more informed decision.

### New Stock Identified Vehicle

A new-stock offer should identify an already built car. Ask where it is, whether it is available, and when it could actually be handed over. Its configuration needs checking against that particular vehicle, including any advertised options. A stock offer can let you assess a specific car, but the label does not eliminate specification errors or guarantee immediate delivery.

### New Order Written Configuration

In contrast, a new order written configuration involves ordering a car that is yet to be built. This allows you to customize the vehicle to your exact specifications, choosing everything from the color to the interior features. While this offers a personalized experience, it also means a longer wait time for delivery. Additionally, the final price may be subject to changes based on the options you select and any potential market fluctuations.

### Used Specific Vehicle

A used-car offer concerns a particular vehicle with its own usage, condition and records. Confirm its present availability and the work or equipment included in the offer. Compare the actual quotation with your alternatives, then arrange appropriate checks of its condition. Neither the price nor the warranty wording can be inferred from the used-car label.

### Demonstrator/Pre-Registered Vehicles

Demonstrator or pre-registered vehicles are often used by dealerships for test drives or have been registered to meet sales targets. These cars typically have low mileage and are nearly new, but they may have been driven by multiple people. When considering such a vehicle, ask about its registration date and usage history to understand its condition better. These vehicles don't fit neatly into the new or used categories, so it's important to evaluate them on their individual merits.

## Establish a Comparable Quotation

To make an informed decision, you need to establish a comparable quotation for each vehicle you're considering. This involves several key steps:

### Exact Vehicle/Configuration

Begin by ensuring that you have detailed information about the exact vehicle or configuration you're interested in. This includes the make, model, year, and any specific features or options. Having this information allows you to compare like-for-like offers accurately.

### Cash Price Basis

When comparing offers, focus on the cash price basis. This is the total amount you would pay if you were to purchase the vehicle outright, without financing. This figure provides a clear comparison point between different vehicles and sellers.

### Included/Excluded Items

Clarify what is included and excluded in the quoted price. Some offers might include additional items such as extended warranties, service packages, or accessories, while others may not. Understanding these details helps you assess the true value of each offer.

### Required Conditions

Identify any conditions that must be met for the offer to be valid. This could include trade-in requirements, financing through the dealership, or specific purchase deadlines. Knowing these conditions ensures there are no surprises later in the process.

### Timing

Consider the timing of each offer. Some vehicles may be available for immediate delivery, while others might require a waiting period. Understanding the timing helps you plan accordingly and avoid any inconvenience.

## Confirm Availability for Every Candidate

Availability is a critical factor in the car-buying process. It's important to confirm the availability of each vehicle you're considering to avoid any unexpected delays.

### Stock/Used Not Automatically Immediate

Even if a vehicle is listed as stock or used, it doesn't guarantee immediate availability. The car might still be in transit or undergoing final preparations. Always verify the actual status with the seller.

### Order Estimate vs. Commitment

For new order vehicles, distinguish between an estimated delivery date and a committed one. An estimate is subject to change, while a commitment is a more reliable indication of when you can expect the car.

### Ask Which Date Reflects Actual Handover

Finally, ask the seller which date reflects the actual handover of the vehicle to you. This is the date when you can take possession of the car, not just when it's built or arrives at the dealership. Confirming this date helps manage your expectations and plan for any necessary arrangements.

### Analyzing the Costs

- **A New Order**: This option has a quoted price of 28,000 EUR with an additional 300 EUR for delivery, bringing the known subtotal to 28,300 EUR. However, this does not include any unquoted taxes, registration fees, or insurance costs. The total cost could be higher once these factors are considered.

- **B Used**: The used car is priced at 24,500 EUR, with 600 EUR allocated for known necessary work and 200 EUR for travel expenses, totaling a known subtotal of 25,300 EUR. Like the new car, this subtotal excludes taxes, registration, and insurance, which could significantly impact the final cost.

- **C New Stock**: With a quoted price of 28,000 EUR, the delivery cost remains unknown, making it impossible to determine a known subtotal. This option requires further clarification on delivery costs and other potential fees.

### Cost Difference

The difference between the known subtotals of options A and B is 3,000 EUR. This difference highlights the potential savings of purchasing a used car, though it is essential to consider the unresolved costs that could affect the final decision.

## Same Advertised Price Can Mean Different Offers

While options A and C both have an advertised price of 28,000 EUR, the offers are not identical due to differences in delivery, timing, and equipment details.

- **Delivery**: Option A includes a known delivery cost of 300 EUR, while option C's delivery cost is still unknown, which could affect the overall price.

- **Timing**: The delivery time for option A is quoted at 12 weeks, which may not meet the buyer's needs if they require a car sooner. Option C's delivery time is unknown, necessitating further inquiry.

- **Equipment**: The specific equipment and features included in each offer may vary, impacting the value and suitability of the car for the buyer's needs.

## Change One Assumption

When evaluating these options, it's important to consider how changing one assumption can impact the decision.

- **Timing Requirement**: If the buyer needs a car within four weeks, option A's 12-week delivery time is not feasible unless the buyer arranges temporary transportation. This could involve renting a car or using public transport, adding to the overall cost and complexity.

- **Option B's Timing**: The used car option claims a two-week availability, contingent on the condition and completion of necessary work. This makes it a potentially viable choice if the condition aligns with the buyer's expectations and the work can be completed on schedule.

- **Clarification Needed for Option C**: Without knowing the delivery time and additional costs for option C, it is difficult to assess its suitability. Buyers should seek clarification on these points before making a decision.

### Conclusion

Before choosing a car, it's essential to confirm all critical factors, including timing, delivery costs, and additional fees. Each option presents unique challenges and opportunities, and understanding these can help buyers make an informed decision that aligns with their needs and budget.

## Compare Equipment and Condition on Evidence

Compare the equipment and condition using evidence tied to the offer. For an order, request a **written quotation with the exact configuration**. Check which options it includes, which remain subject to confirmation, and whether the seller has recorded your essential requirements. A model brochure describes possibilities; it does not establish what your quotation includes.

For a stock vehicle, request the **specification of that particular car** and confirm its availability separately. A built car is not necessarily ready for immediate handover. Match the document to the seller’s vehicle reference and ask about any differences between the advertisement, photos and quotation.

For a used car, separate **installed** from **working**. Ask to see essential functions demonstrated on the actual vehicle. Record what you observed and what remains outside your ability to assess. A viewing or test drive may reveal a concern, but an uneventful drive does not establish mechanical condition; arrange an independent professional assessment when condition is important to the decision.

Remember, do not assume that new cars are defect-free or equipped with the latest technology. Manufacturing errors can occur, and some features may be outdated due to production cycles. Always verify the equipment and condition through direct evidence rather than assumptions.

## Compare Warranties by Wording, Not Years

When comparing warranties, focus on the specific wording rather than the duration. Different providers offer varying levels of coverage, so it's crucial to understand the details. Examine the **provider** of the warranty, whether it's the manufacturer or a third-party company. This can affect the reliability and ease of the claims process.

Check the **start date** and **end date** in the applicable documents. Do not assume that coverage starts again when you buy the car. Ask which document establishes the remaining term and whether it applies to you and the specific vehicle.

Review **covered items and exclusions** together. Choose the items that concern you and find the clauses that address them. Record limits or unresolved wording rather than interpreting the word “comprehensive” as a promise that every problem is covered. Ask the provider to clarify a clause that could change your decision.

Read the **conditions** attached to the warranty, including any documentation or maintenance requirements. Record what the actual wording requires and who can answer questions. Do not infer a restriction from a seller’s shorthand or treat a commercial warranty as the full statement of your legal rights.

Evaluate the **transferability** of the warranty. If you plan to sell the car before the warranty expires, a transferable warranty can be a selling point. Check the process and any fees involved in transferring the warranty to a new owner.

Finally, understand the **claim process from documents**. A straightforward process with clear instructions and minimal paperwork is preferable. Complicated procedures can delay repairs and increase frustration.

Manufacturer and commercial warranties are separate from applicable consumer rights. Verify these rights with local authorities to ensure you are fully informed. For more information, you can visit the [EU consumer guarantees page](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/consumers/shopping/guarantees/index_en.htm).

## Separate Routine Estimates from Unknowns

When budgeting for a car, separate routine cost estimates from unknowns. Obtain **energy, insurance, and maintenance** quotes from reliable sources. Use consistent periods for comparison, such as annual costs, to ensure accuracy.

For energy costs, consider the type of fuel or electricity the car uses. Request quotes from local energy providers or use online calculators to estimate fuel or charging expenses. Record these estimates for comparison.

Insurance quotes should be based on your personal driving history and the specific vehicle. Contact multiple insurance providers to get a range of quotes. Ensure that each quote covers the same level of protection for an accurate comparison.

Request estimates for the scheduled work and known needs of each specific candidate. A warranty is not evidence that routine maintenance is free. Keep documented work due soon separate from unknown future repairs, and record the scope and date of each estimate so the figures remain comparable.

Record any **unknowns** until you can obtain a quote. This includes potential repair costs for used cars or future changes in energy prices. Keeping track of these unknowns helps you prepare for unexpected expenses.

## Copyable Seller Quotation Request

When requesting a quotation from a seller, it's crucial to gather comprehensive information to make an informed decision. Below are templates for both new and used car inquiries.

### New Car Order Request

- **Identity and Configuration**: Clearly specify the make, model, trim, and any additional features or packages you desire. Example: "I am interested in a new [Make] [Model] with [Trim] and [Feature Package]."
- **Current Availability**: Ask if the specific configuration is available for immediate delivery or if there is a waiting period. Example: "Is this configuration available in stock, or what is the estimated delivery time?"
- **All Required Price Conditions**: Request a breakdown of the total price, including any discounts, taxes, and incentives. Example: "Please provide the total price, including any applicable discounts and taxes."
- **Itemised Fees**: Ask for a detailed list of all fees, such as registration, delivery, and preparation charges. Example: "Could you itemise all additional fees, including registration and delivery?"
- **Documents for History/Equipment**: Request documentation that confirms the car's features and any pre-delivery inspections. Example: "Please include documentation confirming the equipment and any pre-delivery inspections."
- **Warranty Terms**: Inquire about the warranty coverage, including duration and what it covers. Example: "What are the warranty terms, including duration and coverage details?"
- **Next Date**: Confirm the next steps and any deadlines for placing an order. Example: "What is the deadline for confirming this order?"

### Used Car Follow-Up Request

- **Identity and Configuration**: Specify the car's make, model, year, and any specific features you are interested in. Example: "I am interested in the used [Make] [Model] [Year] with [Features]."
- **Current Availability**: Confirm the car's availability and whether it can be reserved. Example: "Is this car currently available, and can it be reserved?"
- **All Required Price Conditions**: Request the total price, including any applicable taxes and fees. Example: "Please provide the total price, including taxes and any additional fees."
- **Itemised Fees**: Ask for a breakdown of all associated costs, such as transfer fees or reconditioning charges. Example: "Could you provide an itemised list of all additional fees?"
- **Documents for History/Equipment**: Request the vehicle's service history, accident reports, and equipment list. Example: "Please provide the full service history, accident reports, and equipment list."
- **Warranty Terms**: Inquire about any remaining warranty or additional warranty options. Example: "What warranty options are available, and what do they cover?"
- **Next Date**: Confirm the next steps and any deadlines for purchase. Example: "What is the deadline for confirming this purchase?"

## FAQs

### Is a New or Used Car Always Cheaper?

There is no useful answer without comparable offers. List the quoted vehicle price and known additional costs for each candidate, keeping unknown costs separate. The fictional worksheet above compares those subtotals; it does not forecast depreciation, repairs or financing costs. Request the missing figures before treating an advertised difference as a saving.

### Is Stock Ready?

Stock availability varies by dealer and model. Always confirm with the seller whether the car is available for immediate delivery or if there is a waiting period.

### Is a Longer Warranty Better?

A longer term alone does not establish better coverage. Compare the provider, remaining term, covered items, exclusions and conditions in the actual documents. A claim that matters to you may be excluded from either offer; resolve that question before treating warranty length as a deciding advantage.

### Is a Used Car at New Price Bad?

A used car priced similarly to a new one may offer additional features or be in high demand. Evaluate the car's condition, mileage, and included features to determine if the price is justified.
